About the Community

California's oldest city, one of its most diverse

Founded in 1777 as a Spanish farming pueblo, San Jose grew into the tenth-largest city in the country and the economic and cultural anchor of Silicon Valley — and one of the most ethnically diverse cities in America, home to the largest Vietnamese population of any city outside Vietnam. With over 1,000,000 residents spread across nearly 180 square miles, its neighborhoods vary enormously, from historic Willow Glen to the wooded hills of Almaden Valley.

History

From Spanish pueblo to tech capital

Founded on November 29, 1777, as El Pueblo de San José de Guadalupe, San Jose was the first civilian settlement in Spanish California, established to farm for military outposts in San Francisco and Monterey. It served as California's first state capital briefly after statehood in 1850.

A farming and canning town for over a century, San Jose was transformed by aggressive postwar annexation and the rise of the semiconductor industry, adopting the motto "Capital of Silicon Valley" in 1988. It surpassed San Francisco in population in 1990.
