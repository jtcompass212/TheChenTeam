About the Community

A planned town built entirely around water

Developer T. Jack Foster transformed a Bay salt marsh into Foster City in the 1960s, threading it with canals and a central lagoon that many homes back directly onto with their own small docks. It's also one of the safest cities in the country, averaging just one homicide per decade.

History

Built from salt marsh, by design

Where San Mateo, Burlingame, and San Francisco Bay meet, developer T. Jack Foster saw an opportunity in what was then tidal salt marsh. Through the 1960s, his company engineered a fully planned residential community around a man-made central lagoon and a network of connecting canals.

Foster City incorporated on April 27, 1971, and today more than 80% of its total area remains water — a defining feature almost no other Peninsula city shares.
