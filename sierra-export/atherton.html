About the Community

Privacy, acreage, and Silicon Valley at the door

Atherton has no commercial zoning of any kind — residents drive to neighboring Menlo Park or Redwood City for dining and shopping. What it offers instead is space: hedged, one-acre-plus lots under a dense oak and redwood canopy, minutes from Stanford University and the heart of Silicon Valley. It's consistently ranked among the most expensive ZIP codes in the country.

History

From Fair Oaks to Atherton

The area was known as Fair Oaks in the 1860s, a flag stop on the Southern Pacific line for the wealthy San Franciscans who summered here. When it came time to incorporate in 1923, the name was already taken by a town near Sacramento — so residents honored Faxon Dean Atherton, one of the peninsula's first large landowners, instead.

Large estates were subdivided through the 1920s–50s under a strict one-acre minimum, a rule that still defines the town today. Holbrook-Palmer Park, donated to Atherton in 1958, remains its only public park.
