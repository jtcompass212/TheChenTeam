About the Community

An estate town, by design and by law

Hillsborough's zoning has stayed remarkably consistent since incorporation: a half-acre minimum lot size, a 2,500-square-foot minimum home size, and no commercial zoning of any kind — there are no stores, restaurants, or office buildings within town limits, only homes, schools, a golf course, a country club, and small parks. That consistency, paired with winding, hilly, largely unmarked streets, has made Hillsborough one of the most private and consistently wealthy small towns in the country.

Schools

Education in Hillsborough

Hillsborough is served by its own Hillsborough City School District (K–8) — three elementary schools feeding a single, highly regarded middle school. Hillsborough has no public high school of its own; students are zoned to San Mateo Union High School District, most commonly Burlingame High.

History

Incorporated to stay exactly as it was

Hillsborough sits on land once part of Rancho San Mateo, purchased in 1846 by William Davis Merry Howard, whose family named the area after his father's hometown of Hillsborough, New Hampshire. After the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, wealthy descendants of mining and railroad fortunes built grand estates here, drawn by the semi-rural setting so close to the city.

Residents voted 60 to 1 to incorporate on May 5, 1910, specifically to resist annexation by neighboring cities and protect the town's low-density, estate-oriented character — a framework of half-acre minimum lots and zero commercial zoning that has held, largely unchanged, for over a century.
