About the Community

Small-town character, top-ranked schools

San Carlos sits between Belmont and Redwood City, built around a genuinely walkable downtown on Laurel Street lined with boutiques, cafes, and family-owned restaurants. The San Carlos School District consistently ranks among the top K–8 public districts in California — often cited as the main reason families choose it over neighboring Peninsula cities at comparable price points.

History

From railroad stop to "City of Good Living"

Named for the Spanish mission of San Carlos Borromeo, the town grew slowly until the arrival of the Southern Pacific Railroad. Frederick Drake, later known as "the Father of San Carlos," paved the streets, formed the sanitary district, and helped push the city toward incorporation in 1925 — he's also credited with coining the "City of Good Living" nickname that stuck.

The population grew from about 600 in 1925 to 5,000 by 1941, and electronics pioneers like Litton Engineering Laboratories and Ampex later established some of the city's earliest tech-industry roots.
