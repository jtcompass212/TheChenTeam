Belmont Homes & Real Estate

Belmont ranks among the most competitive real estate markets on the Peninsula — homes here typically draw multiple offers and go under contract in about two weeks, shaped by a distinctive hillside terrain that ranges from flat, walkable neighborhoods to steep, view-lined ridgelines. Incorporated in 1926 and home to roughly 28,000 residents, Belmont pairs a genuine small-town feel with quick access to major tech employers, top-rated schools, and miles of nearby open space.

About the Community

A hillside town with an unusual amount of terrain

Belmont — a variation of the French beaumont, "beautiful mountains" — packs dramatic elevation change into just 4.6 square miles, from flat, sea-level streets to steep, view-exposed ridgelines. Unlike some Peninsula neighbors, Belmont doesn't have one traditional downtown strip; instead, its character shows up in Ralston Hall, its wooded parks, and a housing stock that varies block by block depending on the slope.

History

From stagecoach stop to Gilded Age estate

Settled in 1850 as a stagecoach station, Belmont's most famous early resident was William Ralston, a founder of the Bank of California, who in 1866 transformed an Italian count's hillside villa into Ralston Hall — an opulent Italianate estate that anticipated features later built into his San Francisco Palace Hotel.

Belmont incorporated in 1926. For decades before World War II, it was known as the country's "chrysanthemum center" for its flower-shipping industry — a distinction it lost after its Japanese American flower growers were forcibly removed from the area during the war.
