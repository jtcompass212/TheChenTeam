About the Community

A small city with a big bridge and older roots than most

Newark takes its unusual name from Newark Castle in Port Glasgow, Scotland, chosen by an early settler after a post office opened here in 1878. Today it's the third-largest U.S. city named Newark, an enclave completely surrounded by Fremont, and still home to Cargill's working salt evaporation ponds — a visible reminder of an industry that predates the city itself by well over a century.

History

Salt, steel, and a Scottish castle's name

Long before European settlement, the Ohlone lived throughout the area, followed by Spanish missionaries from Mission San José. A post office opened in 1878, and early settler J. Barr Robertson named the community after Newark Castle in Port Glasgow, Scotland — making it, today, the third-largest U.S. city named Newark, after the ones in New Jersey and Ohio.

Newark incorporated on September 22, 1955. Its economy historically centered on salt production (still active today through Cargill), along with manufacturing — Wedgewood stoves and Peterbilt trucks were both built here for decades before the city's shift toward its current mix of retail, logistics, and residential growth.
