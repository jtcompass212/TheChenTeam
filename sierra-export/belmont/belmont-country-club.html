Belmont Country Club Homes & Real Estate

Named for a 9-hole golf course and clubhouse that developers built here in 1925 and lost to the 1929 crash, Belmont Country Club is one of the city's largest and most varied neighborhoods — its old fairways were subdivided into homes after World War II, leaving a mix of orderly 1950s–60s ranch blocks and custom hillside estates along winding streets named for Belmont pioneers like Sharon, Cipriani, and Pullman. Bordered by San Mateo to the north and a looping Ralston Avenue to the south and west, it's a short drive from Belmont's Caltrain station and downtown.
