About the Community

The Peninsula's largest city, at its center

San Mateo blends a lively downtown with quiet, tree-lined residential pockets on either side of it. Families are drawn to strong public schools and a genuine mix of housing, from grand San Mateo Park estates to modest starter homes in Shoreview; commuters love sitting right at the junction of Highway 101 and 92, with Caltrain and SFO both minutes away.

History

Named for a creek, built by a railroad

San Mateo takes its name from a creek christened by Spanish missionaries traveling El Camino Real; by 1793, an outpost of Mission San Francisco de Asís stood nearby, on ground now marked as a California Historical Landmark.

Passenger rail reached San Mateo in October 1863, and the railroad's own director chose the depot site that became downtown. The city incorporated in 1894 and has since grown into the most populous city in San Mateo County.
