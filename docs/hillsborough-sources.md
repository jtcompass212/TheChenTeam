# Hillsborough neighborhood pages — sources

Prose on the 15 Hillsborough pages is written for this site. Sources below were
used for facts only. No wording was carried across.

## Town history

- [Burlingame Properties — A Brief History of Hillsborough](https://burlingameproperties.com/resources/a-brief-history-of-hillsborough)
  — William Davis Merry Howard bought the 6,500-acre Rancho San Mateo in 1848
  for $25,000; John McLaren planted the eucalyptus; in 1893 Francis Newlands
  turned family dairy land into luxury real estate, commissioning A. Page Brown
  for five grand "cottages" and founding the Burlingame Country Club, the first
  American country club west of the Mississippi. Residents voted 60 to 1 for
  incorporation on 5 May 1910, fearing annexation by Burlingame; a founding
  regulation prohibited sidewalks. No commercial zoning, half-acre minimum lots.
  After the Second World War, labour shortages made the great houses
  impossible to staff and owners sold acreage to developers — which is where
  most of today's neighborhoods came from.

## Estates

- [Hillsborough Blog — history and architecture](https://hillsboroughblog.com/History)
  and [Homes.com — Hillsborough neighborhoods](https://www.homes.com/neighborhood-search/hillsborough-ca/)
  — Carolands, the 98-room chateau completed 1916 for Pullman heiress Harriett
  Pullman Carolan, its 554 acres subdivided through the 1940s–50s, the house
  restored by Charles and Ann Johnson in 1998 and now shown by the Carolands
  Foundation on docent tours by lottery; Skyfarm, a gift from William H.
  Crocker to his sister-in-law, later the centrepiece of the Nueva School;
  Uplands, now Crystal Springs Uplands School; Tobin Clark's 1931
  Cotswold-style "House-on-Hill", still a private residence.
- The town recognises fourteen designated neighborhoods. The map also carries
  **Burlingame Hills**, which is neither Hillsborough nor Burlingame but
  unincorporated San Mateo County between them — its page says so.

## Schools

- [Hillsborough City School District](https://www.hcsdk8.org/welcome-to-hcsd/about-hcsd/welcome-to-hcsd)
  — South Hillsborough built 1917; North and West Hillsborough in the early
  1950s; William H. Crocker Middle School opened 1959. The district is K–8;
  there is no high school in Hillsborough.

**Note:** school cards name schools serving the town. They do not assert
attendance-boundary assignment, which was not verified per neighborhood.

## No year-over-year figures anywhere in Hillsborough

This is the only city on the site where deltas are suppressed town-wide rather
than page by page, and the town's own numbers are the argument. On the same
quarter its neighborhoods returned **+57.9%, +51.7%, +32.5% and −50%**.

The 8-sale threshold used everywhere else was calibrated on normally-priced
neighborhoods. Hillsborough medians run $4.3M to $9.0M, so a quarter with nine
closed sales is nine estates — and which nine decides the median far more than
the market does. Homeplace cleared the threshold on 9 sales at +51.7%; that is
the same failure San Mateo Park showed at +46.9% on 10, which we suppressed
individually.

Medians, sale counts and windows still publish on every page. Only the
percentage arrow is withheld, and each page states why.

`parrot-drive-area` publishes nothing at all: Compass records at most one sale
a year against the name.
