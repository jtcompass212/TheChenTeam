# San Bruno neighborhood pages — sources

Prose on the 17 San Bruno pages is written for this site. Sources below were
used for facts only. No wording was carried across.

## Neighborhoods

San Bruno divides cleanly into flatland postwar tracts and hillside
raised-ranch neighborhoods, and the pages are written around that split.

- [Homes.com — San Bruno](https://www.homes.com/local-guide/san-bruno-ca/)
  and [Burlingame Properties — San Bruno](https://burlingameproperties.com/communities/san-bruno)
  — Huntington Park, San Bruno Park and Lomita Park as flatland tracts from the
  late 1940s and early 1950s with compact houses on modest lots; Crestmoor,
  Rollingwood, Mills Park, Monte Verde and Portola Highlands as hillside
  neighborhoods of larger mid-century homes, many raised so the lower level
  meets the street at grade.
- Crestmoor built on former ranch land west of I-280 from the early 1950s,
  raised ranches and split-levels between 1950 and 1970 on curving streets.
- Mills Park conceived pre-war as an entry-level suburb for people leaving San
  Francisco, halted by the war, resumed afterwards for returning veterans.
- Rollingwood's 1950s–60s ranch houses; Portola Highlands' 1960s–70s raised
  ranches.
- [Shelter Creek](https://www.homes.com/neighborhood-search/san-bruno-ca/) as
  the city's large condominium community.

## Schools

- Capuchino High School opened 11 September 1950 (San Mateo Union High School
  District). Elementary and middle grades are run by the San Bruno Park School
  District.

**Note:** school cards name schools serving the city. They do not assert
attendance-boundary assignment, which was not verified per neighborhood.

## Five pages publish no market figures

**Not residential:**

- [Bayhill](https://www.homes.com/local-guide/san-bruno-ca/bay-hill-neighborhood/)
  — an office district in the middle of the city; YouTube is among the
  employers based there. No housing stock.
- [Tanforan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanforan) — a shopping center on the
  site of the racetrack that operated from 1899.
- [Golden Gate National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_National_Cemetery)
  — established by the War Department in 1939 as space ran short at the
  Presidio.

**In Compass but unattributed:** Belle Air North and Downtown San Bruno are not
in the Compass neighborhood taxonomy. Sales on those streets are recorded under
adjoining neighborhoods — Belle Air Park in the first case. Both pages say so
rather than borrowing a citywide median.

## Tanforan

The Tanforan page states the site's history directly. In 1942, under Executive
Order 9066, the racetrack grounds became the
[Tanforan Assembly Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanforan_Racetrack),
where thousands of Japanese Americans — most of them United States citizens —
were detained before removal to incarceration camps inland. The track burned
down in 1964 and the shopping center built on the site includes a memorial.

Handling this required a fix to the page generator. Every page ended with
"Below you'll find current homes for sale in ___, updated throughout the day."
On a page about an incarceration site — and on a cemetery, a golf course and a
wildlife refuge — that line is wrong. Non-residential pages no longer carry it.
