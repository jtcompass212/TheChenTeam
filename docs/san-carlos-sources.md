# San Carlos neighborhood pages — sources

Prose on the 6 San Carlos pages is written for this site. These sources were
used for the underlying facts only — founding dates, build eras, boundaries,
school and park names. No wording was carried across.

## Neighborhood history

- [LookyLoo — San Carlos neighborhoods](https://lookyloomove.com/locations/san-carlos/neighborhoods)
  and [Burlingame Properties — San Carlos](https://burlingameproperties.com/communities/san-carlos)
  — Howard Park's origins with the San Carlos Park Syndicate and Frederick
  Drake, and the 1918 schoolhouse; Beverly Terrace as the largest hill
  neighborhood and Nathaniel Brittan's 1872 octagonal house at 125 Dale Avenue;
  Clearfield Park's position between El Camino Real and Highway 101 and its
  cinder-block postwar stock; Cordes on the Belmont line holding some of the
  city's oldest houses; El Sereno Corte's 1930s lot subdivision down to ~5,000
  square feet; Alder Manor still ranchland as late as 1960, now the largest
  lots in the city.
- The Greater San Francisco Speedway — a 1.25-mile board oval opened in 1921 in
  what is now Clearfield Park, which burned down in June 1922 after four races.

## Schools

- [San Carlos School District — Schools](https://www.scsdk8.org/apps/pages/Schools)
  — an unusual structure worth getting right: TK–3 at Arundel, Brittan Acres,
  Heather and White Oaks; grades 4–5 at Arroyo and Mariposa Upper Elementary;
  grades 6–8 at Central and Tierra Linda Middle.
- [Sequoia Union High School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequoia_Union_High_School_District)
  and [Carlmont High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlmont_High_School)
  — San Carlos has no high school of its own; students go to Sequoia Union,
  most often Carlmont, whose campus straddles the San Carlos–Belmont line.

**Note:** school cards name schools serving San Carlos. They do not assert
attendance-boundary assignment, which was not verified per neighborhood.

## Market data

Compass Market Insights, pulled 24 August 2026. All six names were screened
against the region-wide sentinel first (see `data/market/README.md`) and all
six resolve to real neighborhood filters.

San Carlos has the strongest neighborhood-level volume of any city on the site
— 5 to 23 closed single-family sales in 2026-Q2 — so every page reports a
current quarter rather than falling back to a year.

Two pages lose their year-over-year arrow under the 8-sale threshold:
Clearfield Park (6 sales, and its prior-year quarter was too thin to compare
against at all) and Howard Park, whose +29.5% rested on 5 sales.
