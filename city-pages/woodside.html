About the Community

Old West character, Silicon Valley proximity

Woodside supplied the redwood lumber that built San Francisco — twice, before the Gold Rush and again after the 1906 earthquake — and the town has stayed true to its wooded, rural roots ever since. Residents keep horses on a town-maintained network of trails, and the tiny downtown is intentionally kept to a general store, a saloon, and a couple of restaurants, just minutes from Sand Hill Road.

History

The town that built San Francisco

In 1849, a 20-year-old named Mathias Parkhurst bought 127 acres of timberland and named it Woodside. Its sawmills supplied the redwood that built San Francisco — first before the Gold Rush, then again after the 1906 earthquake — before wealthy San Franciscans began building country estates here, including Filoli, designed by architect Willis Polk.

Residents voted to incorporate in 1956, specifically to keep control over zoning and prevent the town from urbanizing — a decision that still shapes Woodside's rural, low-density character today.
