About the Community

"A City in the Sun," built for commuters

Millbrae takes its name from Darius Ogden Mills, a Gold Rush-era banker who built a country estate here in the 1860s (Mills + the Scottish "brae," meaning hillside). Today the city sits directly on top of one of the Bay Area's few combined BART and Caltrain stations, with San Francisco International Airport just to the northeast.

History

From country estate to commuter town

In the 1860s, Gold Rush financier Darius Ogden Mills bought land from the Sánchez family — original Mexican land grantees of Rancho Buri Buri — to build a country estate he called Millbrae, blending his surname with the Scottish word for a hillside. The mansion burned down in 1954, and the land was later redeveloped into Mills High School, Spring Valley Elementary, and Peninsula Hospital.

Millbrae incorporated on January 14, 1948. Its economy historically centered on farming, flower growing, dairy, and porcelain manufacturing before shifting toward the commuter-driven community it is today.
