About the Community

Where the university meets the valley

Palo Alto is built around two walkable downtowns — University Avenue and California Avenue — and bordered by Stanford to the west. Its neighborhoods range from Victorian-era Professorville to the Eichler tracts of Greenmeadow, and nearly a third of the city's 26 square miles remains open space, including the Baylands Nature Preserve along the Bay.

History

Named for a redwood, built for a university

Palo Alto takes its name from El Palo Alto, a coast redwood still standing where the Portolá expedition camped in 1769. In 1894, Leland and Jane Stanford founded the town alongside Stanford University, in memory of their son; the original town banned saloons within a mile of campus.

Palo Alto annexed the neighboring town of Mayfield in 1925, and in 1939 two Stanford graduates started a company in a garage on Addison Avenue — Hewlett-Packard, now commemorated as the birthplace of Silicon Valley.
