About the Community

From racetrack town to tech neighbor

San Bruno incorporated in 1914 and grew up around the Tanforan Racetrack, once one of the Peninsula's most prominent landmarks. Today the city is home to YouTube's headquarters, along with easy access to San Bruno Mountain State Park, Sweeney Ridge, and Junipero Serra County Park.

History

From a racetrack to a residential city

San Bruno takes its name from Bruno Heceta, the Spanish explorer who named the mountain in 1775; the area was originally part of the Rancho Buri Buri land grant. In 1911, aviator Eugene Ely made history at Tanforan Racetrack, taking off and successfully landing on a ship in San Francisco Bay for the first time.

San Bruno incorporated in 1914, largely to get its streets paved, and grew quickly through the 20th century. During World War II, Tanforan Racetrack served as a temporary assembly center where Japanese Americans were held before being sent to larger internment camps — a difficult chapter in the city's history that's still acknowledged locally today. The racetrack was redeveloped as The Shops at Tanforan in 1971.
