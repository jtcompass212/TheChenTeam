About the Community

From "the smokestack capital" to biotech's birthplace

South San Francisco built its identity as "The Industrial City" in the early 20th century, a motto still spelled out in giant letters on Sign Hill. When Genentech opened here in 1976, the city earned a second nickname — the "Birthplace of Biotechnology" — and now hosts more than 2,800 businesses alongside its residential neighborhoods.

History

From Baden to "The Industrial City"

Originally agricultural land known as Baden, South San Francisco incorporated in 1908 after a dispute with San Mateo County over a proposed smelter on San Bruno Point. Steel mills and other heavy industry soon followed, and by the 1920s the city called itself the "smokestack capital of the Peninsula" — a motto immortalized in 1923 by giant hillside letters on Sign Hill that still stand today.

The city's population grew from 4,411 in 1920 to over 66,000 residents today. Genentech's arrival in 1976 marked the start of a decades-long shift from heavy industry toward biotechnology and life sciences.
